How Attendance decides what Payroll will pay

A module that's being built, and the module that already speaks its language

Payroll is under active development. Attendance already computes, today, in running code, every fact the Payroll module will use to price a payslip — who was paid for a full day, a half day, or nothing; how much overtime was approved; what freezes at lock and why it can never quietly change afterward. This document is a companion to HRMS_POLICY_CLIENT_REVIEW.html (Attendance & Leave): that document explains the rules that produce a day's status, this one explains what happens to that status once money is involved.

02

Executive Summary

Payroll itself is under active development — the pay run, payslip, and salary formula engine are being built now. What is already built and running today is the entire fact contract Payroll will consume: a per-day paid/unpaid fraction, approved-only overtime minutes, a comp-off-earned flag, and a per-employee period summary that freezes permanently the moment its attendance period locks. Attendance never computes a rupee amount — that boundary is enforced by design (ADR-0006, ADR-0011), not by omission.

The one gap that matters most before this contract is trusted with real payroll runs: once a period is locked, the only way to fix a mistake is to reopen the entire period. There is no per-record correction, and the "arrears" object the architecture names as the intended long-term fix has not been designed or built (Section 14). Everything else in this document — the pay-fraction table, the overtime approval gate, the comp-off fork, the period-summary freeze — is already running code, verified directly against apps/api/src/hrms/modules/attendance/, not aspirational documentation.

03

How to Use This Document

First pass

Read Sections 4–12 as a straight explanation: what Attendance hands off, in what shape, at what moment, and why. No client decision is required to understand this part — it describes what the system already does.

Second pass — decisions

Section 14 is the only part of this document asking something of you. It carries forward the two payroll-relevant decisions already open in the Attendance & Leave guide (fixing a locked mistake, and whether OT still pays on a leave day) and adds one new one specific to this boundary: the default that decides pay for an incomplete day nobody resolved before lock.

This document does not re-derive Attendance's own policy rules (grace minutes, late bands, half-day thresholds, leave sandwich rules, etc.) — those live in HRMS_POLICY_CLIENT_REVIEW.html. This document starts at the point those rules have already produced a day's status, and follows only what happens next on the way to Payroll.

04

Where Payroll Fits Today

The product's own architecture (Doc 03 §6, Workforce & Attendance Architecture §22) is explicit that Payroll is a future module and Attendance's job is to publish facts, not money. That boundary already shapes the code that exists today, even with no Payroll module to receive it.

Attendance

Built. Punches, policies, overtime, regularization, locking, and a payroll-facing fact snapshot are all implemented and tested.

→ → → facts only, no rupees
(ADR-0006, ADR-0011)

Payroll

Being built now. The pay run, payslip, salary formula builder, and statutory engine are in active development, against the contract this document describes.

Two architecture decisions already lock the shape of that future boundary, and are worth knowing before reading the rest of this document:

ADR-0006 — Money as integer minor units

Wherever money eventually appears, it will be stored as integer paise/cents, never a float. Attendance never stores money at all, so this doesn't touch Attendance directly — but it is why Attendance's fields are always counts, minutes, and fractions, never currency.

ADR-0005 — Payroll inputs snapshotted; finalised runs immutable

A pay run will copy Attendance's period-end facts into its own frozen snapshot before computing. Once a run is finalised, its numbers are permanent; any later fix is a new arrears entry in a future run, never an edit to history. Section 12 covers what this means for Attendance's own lock.

05

How Attendance Works — Recap

Full detail lives in HRMS_POLICY_CLIENT_REVIEW.html §5. This is only the part that matters for what reaches Payroll.

Every employee, every calendar day, resolves to exactly one attendance_status, computed from punches, the assigned Attendance Policy's bands, and (where applicable) an approved leave day:

StatusMeaningFeeds Payroll as…
PRESENTFollowed schedule in fullFull day paid
HALF_DAYHalf present (hours or AM/PM split)Half day paid, half day loss-of-pay (unless a paid half-day leave covers the rest)
ABSENTExpected and not present; not covered by leaveFull loss-of-pay
LEAVEAn approved leave application covers the expected portionPaid or unpaid according to the leave type's own paid flag (Section 10)
WFH / ON_DUTYWorking, away from a fixed punch locationFull day paid, same as Present
NOT_EXPECTEDCalendar says no work was due (holiday, weekly off, shutdown) and none occurredFull day paid if the calendar day itself is a paid one
INCOMPLETEMissing punch, missing shift, or a config error — not yet a pay factNothing, until resolved or forced at lock time (Section 7)

Two dimensions are tracked in parallel and never conflated: calendar_day_type (what the calendar expected — working day, holiday, weekly off) and attendance_status (what actually happened). Payroll receives both, plus a set of boolean flags (worked_on_holiday, is_late, is_regularized, and so on) — see Section 6.

The Daily Pay Facts Attendance Publishes

This is the exact field list the architecture (§22.1) commits Attendance to publishing per employee, per day, once that day is locked. Every field on this list already exists on the AttendanceDay record in the running system.

FieldWhat it tells Payroll
employee_id, attendance_dateWhich employee, which day
calendar_day_typeWorking / Weekly Off / Holiday / Special Working / Shutdown
attendance_statusThe primary state from Section 5
paid_day_fraction, lop_fractionHow much of the day is paid vs. loss-of-pay — see Section 7
worked_minutes, unpaid_break_minutes, paid_break_minutesActual worked time, net of breaks, by paid/unpaid category
ot_minutes_detected, ot_minutes_approvedOvertime candidate vs. what an approver actually signed off — only the second is payable (Section 8)
ot_classificationNORMAL / WEEKLY_OFF / HOLIDAY / NIGHT — which of Payroll's own rate rows should price this OT
configured_multiplierInformational only — the multiplier this tenant configured on the Overtime Policy. Payroll may use it or price OT its own way; Attendance doesn't enforce it as money.
comp_off_minutes_earnedMinutes earned as time-off-in-lieu instead of OT pay — a Leave fact, not a Payroll one (Section 9)
worked_on_holiday, worked_on_weekly_offFlags Payroll's bonus/premium rules may key off
late_minutes, early_out_minutes, late_count_contributedRaw minutes and whether this occurrence counts toward a monthly late-to-half-day counter
penalty_codes[], bonus_codes[]Flags such as LATE_TO_HALF — codes only, never amounts. Pricing a code into rupees is a future Payroll salary-component job.
is_lockedWhether this day's facts are frozen
computation_snapshot_id / version idsExactly which policy/shift/OT-policy versions produced this row, so a figure from six months ago stays explainable even after policies have since changed

Notice what is not on this list: no salary, no rate, no computed rupee penalty or bonus. Every field above is a count, a minute, a fraction, a code, or an identifier — consistent with ADR-0006 and ADR-0011 (Section 4).

07

How a Day Becomes Paid, Half-Paid, or Loss of Pay

paid_day_fraction and lop_fraction always sum to 1.00, and are derived from attendance_status by a fixed table — verified directly in calculation.py's _paid_lop_fraction():

Statuspaid_day_fractionlop_fraction
PRESENT1.000.00
HALF_DAY0.500.50 (unless a paid half-day leave covers the other half)
ABSENT0.001.00
NOT_EXPECTED (paid shutdown / holiday / weekly off)1.000.00
LEAVEFrom the leave type's own paid flagThe inverse
INCOMPLETE / CONFIG_ERROR, still unresolved0.000.00 — deliberately not sent to Payroll yet

An INCOMPLETE day is the one case that can't simply carry a number all the way to lock — nobody yet knows whether it should be paid. Attendance Policy's own incomplete_at_lock_behaviour field decides what happens the moment the period is finalised:

KEEP_INCOMPLETEBlocks finalization outright — the period cannot close with this day unresolved. Someone must regularize it or otherwise resolve it first.
TREAT_ABSENTThe coded default. At the instant of finalize, an unresolved INCOMPLETE day is rewritten to paid_day_fraction=0.00, lop_fraction=1.00 — full loss of pay, exactly as if the employee had been marked Absent.
TREAT_PRESENTThe opposite default: paid_day_fraction=1.00, lop_fraction=0.00 — full pay for a day nobody ever confirmed what actually happened.

This is a real, money-moving default, not a cosmetic one. Every tenant ships with TREAT_ABSENT unless someone explicitly changes it on the Attendance Policy. An employee who forgets to punch out on the last day before lock, and never gets regularized in time, loses a full day's pay by default — not because anyone decided that for this company, but because that's the factory setting. See Decision 14.3.

08

Overtime → Payroll

Overtime is a separate policy from Attendance Policy. Attendance Policy detects extra worked minutes; Overtime Policy decides whether they count, whether they need approval, and how they're classified for Payroll's own rate table.

Detected vs. approved

ot_minutes_detected is recorded the moment extra minutes are worked. ot_minutes_approved stays at zero, and ot_pending=true, until an approver acts. Payroll only ever consumes approved minutes — OT is never auto-paid without an approval record on file.

Classification, not pricing

Each OT minute is tagged NORMAL, WEEKLY_OFF, HOLIDAY, or NIGHT. A configured multiplier_decimal travels alongside as configured_multiplier, purely informational — Payroll may re-price using its own tables instead.

Not a statutory engine

The multiplier is whatever a company typed in, not a coded assumption (no hardcoded "factory workers get 2×"). Statutory OT interpretation, PF-on-OT, and tax are explicitly out of scope for Attendance — Payroll/Statutory's job, per ADR-0011.

Night OT is shift-flag only, today

A configurable night window exists on the Overtime Policy, but the running code only ever classifies OT as Night when the shift itself is flagged cross-midnight — actual clock time against the window is never checked. This is already an open decision in the Attendance & Leave guide (Decision 7 / PD-ATT-002); it matters here because ot_classification is exactly the field Payroll's rate table will key off.

09

Comp-Off: the Fork Between Pay and Leave

An Overtime Policy can be configured with comp_off_instead_of_pay=true. When it is, approved overtime on that day takes a completely different path — verified in the calculation engine, not just documented intent:

comp_off_instead_of_pay = false

Overtime minutes stay in ot_minutes_approved and travel toward Payroll as payable OT, classified and priced there.

↔ policy switch on
the OT policy version

comp_off_instead_of_pay = true

Attendance emits comp_off_minutes_earned instead. This is a Leave fact, not a Payroll one — it becomes a ledger credit once Leave picks it up, with a configurable expiry (default 90 days, itself data, not code).

Attendance never writes to a leave ledger directly (that boundary belongs to Leave, per ADR-0004) — it only stamps the earned-minutes fact onto the day so nothing is lost, exactly the same non-mutating, fact-publishing posture as everything else in this document.

10

Leave's Effect on the Pay Facts

Leave now exists as a real module (it did not when the original Attendance architecture was written — see the documentation-currency note in the Attendance & Leave guide, PD-DOC-001). The pay-fact contract between the two modules is unchanged from what was originally designed:

SituationEffect on the pay facts
Unpaid leave approved for the dayattendance_status=LEAVE, the leave type's is_paid=false → lop_fraction increases by that leave's fraction, exactly as if the day were partly Absent
Half-day leave + half-day attendanceFractions add; the day can still land on paid_day_fraction=1.00 if the worked half plus the paid leave half cover the whole day
Leave and overtime on the same dayThe engine does not clear detected OT just because the day is also Leave — a day can carry both a LEAVE status and a nonzero OT record. This is an open decision in the Attendance & Leave guide (Decision 1 / HD-006); it is repeated here because it directly decides whether Payroll would ever be asked to pay OT on a day someone was also on leave.
Leave approved after the attendance period is already lockedLeave's approval currently does not check the attendance lock at all — verified: no lock/period check exists anywhere in leave/service.py's approval path. The architecture's stated intent (default refuse, HR exception creates arrears) is still being built on both sides. See Section 14.
11

The Period Summary — Attendance's Handoff to Payroll

Daily facts are useful, but a pay run works in periods. AttendancePeriodSummary is the materialised, per-employee, per-period roll-up the architecture names as the actual payroll handoff (§4.2, §22.2, FR-ATT-018) — and it is real, implemented code today, not a future table.

FieldWhat it rolls up
present_days, absent_days, leave_days, holiday_days, weekly_off_days, half_daysDay-count buckets for the period
lop_daysTotal loss-of-pay days — the single number a simple payroll formula would multiply against a per-day rate
ot_minutes_normal, ot_minutes_weekly_off, ot_minutes_holiday, ot_minutes_nightApproved OT minutes, already split by the classification Payroll's rate table needs
comp_off_minutes_earnedTotal comp-off minutes earned this period (Section 9)
late_countHow many late occurrences counted toward the period, for any late-linked salary rule
worked_minutes_totalRaw total worked time for the period
generated_atWhen this snapshot was produced

Never regenerated after lock. While the period is OPEN, a live aggregate is computed on every request and nothing is stored. The instant the period is finalised (status='CLOSED'), exactly one row is written to this table, inside the same transaction — and it is never touched again. This is the frozen snapshot a future Payroll module will read (ADR-0005). It never carries rupees; penalty and bonus amounts remain entirely Payroll's, referencing the day-level penalty_codes/bonus_codes against salary components that don't exist yet.

12

Locking, Snapshots & Why a Payslip Can't Change Retroactively

ADR-0005 describes the payroll pipeline this system is committing to, even before Payroll is built:

1· Create runLock the period, snapshot the employee list.
2· Snapshot inputsCopy compensation, Attendance's period summary, leave, arrears, loans and statutory master versions into an immutable payrun_input_snapshot.
3· ComputeA pure function of the snapshot — a later change to any master record cannot retroactively alter a run already computed.
4–5· Validate & ReviewAutomated checks, variance against the previous run, and a mandatory human approval gate.
6· FinalisePayslips written, the run frozen permanently. Any later error becomes an arrears/adjustment component in a subsequent run — never an edit to this one.

Attendance's own lock is the piece of this pipeline that already exists and is enforced today. When an HR user with ATTENDANCE.LOCK finalises a period:

  • Finalize fails closed on six typed blocking conditions (unresolved INCOMPLETE days under KEEP_INCOMPLETE, pending regularizations, pending OT approvals, unpublished rosters, config errors, and missing days) — nothing half-finished is ever locked.
  • Every remaining INCOMPLETE day is resolved per Section 7's table, in the same transaction that closes the period.
  • AttendancePeriodSummary is written once, frozen, ready for a future pay run to snapshot.
  • From that moment, no punch, regularization, OT approval, roster change, or leave sync can touch a date inside the closed period — the one rule the Attendance & Leave guide calls out as outranking everything else in the system.

The only way back to mutability is reopening the whole period (ATTENDANCE.UNLOCK, a separate, higher-permission, mandatory-reason action). Reopening does not itself recompute anything — a day someone forgets to manually recompute keeps whatever numbers it had at lock time, indefinitely. There is no per-record correction path, and no "arrears" object connecting a post-lock fix to a future payroll run has been built. See Section 14, Decision 14.1.

13

What Payroll Will Never Get From Attendance

Just as important as what Attendance publishes is what it deliberately never will. This boundary is architectural, not incidental:

Any rupee amount

Not a penalty, not a bonus, not a priced OT hour. Attendance emits codes and fractions; pricing them is Payroll's future formula-builder job.

26-day vs. 30-day vs. calendar-day basis

Attendance's lop_fraction is basis-agnostic. Converting a fraction of a day into a fraction of a month's salary (FR-PAY-008) is entirely Payroll's decision to make.

Statutory OT, PF-on-OT, tax

Explicitly named as Payroll/Statutory's job in the architecture (§22.2), never Attendance's, regardless of how OT is classified.

A leave ledger entry

Attendance may emit a comp-off-earned fact (Section 9), but it never writes to any leave balance itself — that boundary belongs to Leave (ADR-0004).

14

Open Decisions Before Payroll Can Rely On This

Three items. Two are already recorded, unresolved, in the Attendance & Leave guide, repeated here specifically because of their payroll impact. One is new, surfaced by this review.

Carried forward · PD-LOCK-2 · High

Decision 14.1 — Fixing a mistake after a period is locked

Unresolved
Why it matters here specifically

Once Payroll exists, this becomes a payroll question, not just an attendance one: a full reopen after a pay run has already snapshotted the period's facts doesn't just risk stale attendance numbers, it risks a payslip computed from data that later turned out to be wrong, with no defined path for how the correction reaches pay.

Current behaviour

No per-record correction path exists. The only remedy is a full period reopen, manual identification of affected days, manual recompute, and re-lock — entirely independent of whether a payroll run has already read the old numbers.

Recommendation
RecommendedDesign a scoped correction/arrears object jointly with Payroll, before Payroll go-live

The Attendance & Leave guide already recommends this (its Decision 2); we'd add that it cannot be designed on the Attendance side alone — "which pay cycle absorbs a late correction" is inherently a Payroll rule, and ADR-0005 already commits to arrears-only correction once a run is finalised. These two designs need to agree with each other before either ships.

Carried forward · HD-006 · High

Decision 14.2 — Does overtime still pay on a day someone was also on leave?

Unresolved
Why it matters here specifically

The system will happily produce a day with attendance_status=LEAVE and a nonzero ot_minutes_approved. If that's sent to Payroll unchanged, an employee could be paid a full leave day and overtime pay for the same 24 hours. Nothing today stops that combination from reaching the period summary.

Current behaviour

Leave approval does not clear or adjust any already-recorded OT minutes for the same day.

Recommendation
Needs your answerConfirm the intended interaction before this reaches a real pay run

This is the Attendance & Leave guide's Decision 1 — genuinely ambiguous, not a bug, since some companies do intend to pay both (e.g. a half-day leave in the morning, verified overtime in the evening). We surface it here because it's the one open Attendance/Leave decision with a direct rupee consequence.

New · High · Attendance/Payroll boundary

Decision 14.3 — Should "incomplete at lock" default to full loss-of-pay?

Unresolved
The question

Every Attendance Policy ships with incomplete_at_lock_behaviour=TREAT_ABSENT unless someone changes it. Should a company-wide, money-moving default like this ship silently, or should every tenant be required to explicitly confirm it during setup?

Why this needs your answer

This is not a hypothetical edge case — it fires every time an employee has an unresolved missing punch that nobody regularizes before the period locks. Under the default, that day becomes full loss-of-pay automatically, with no separate notification step beyond whatever the finalize validation surfaces. The alternative default, TREAT_PRESENT, has the opposite risk: an employee could be paid in full for a day with no confirmed record of what happened.

Current system behaviour

Silent factory default of TREAT_ABSENT, configurable per Attendance Policy, but not surfaced as a decision anyone is asked to confirm during company setup or policy publish.

Options
A — Keep silent default (current)
Advantages
No workflow change.
Risks
A company may not realise this default exists until an employee disputes a pay deduction traced back to an unresolved punch.
B — Require explicit confirmation at policy publishRecommended
Impact
Publishing an Attendance Policy version surfaces this field with its consequence spelled out, rather than leaving it at whatever the form's default happens to be.
Advantages
Matches this system's existing philosophy of surfacing rather than silently resolving ambiguous defaults (see the Attendance & Leave guide's Hidden Policy Decisions section).
Risks
Minor UX/copy work only; no engine change needed since the field and its behaviour already exist.
Recommendation
RecommendedOption B — keep the default, but stop leaving it silent

We are not recommending a behaviour change to the engine, which already does something reasonable and configurable. We are recommending that "an unresolved punch costs the employee a day's pay by default" stop being something a client discovers after the fact.
