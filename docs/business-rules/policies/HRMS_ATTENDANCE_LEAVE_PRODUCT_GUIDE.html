02

Executive Summary

Attendance and Leave share one foundation: a single 8-level scope-resolution engine that decides which policy applies to a given employee on a given date, reused identically by every policy type in both modules. The two modules meet at exactly one seam — Leave writes an approved day into a table Attendance owns, inside the same transaction as the approval — and stay otherwise independent.

Attendance calculates a daily status — Present, Half-Day, Absent, Leave, On-Duty, or Incomplete — from punches, shift rules, holidays, weekly-offs, breaks and roster overrides, in a fixed eight-step evaluation order, and separately detects overtime from the same punches. Leave manages leave types, balances, accrual, carry-forward, multi-level approval, and comp-off, and hands its final decision to Attendance through that one narrow connection. Both modules refuse to guess when required configuration is missing, rather than silently defaulting an employee to Present or Working.

How this document is organised. Sections 5–6 walk a single employee through exactly how a day's attendance status and a leave application are calculated, step by step, with a worked example each. Sections 7–8 catalogue every configurable policy in each module. Sections 9–11 show which rule wins whenever two policies could both apply to the same day, including every scenario where Attendance and Leave directly interact. Sections 12–14 cover overtime, what happens when a policy changes after records already exist, and what a locked pay period means in practice. Section 15 is a single reference matrix of every precedence rule in one place, for quick lookup during a demo or a later question.

91 policies configurable

54 in Attendance, 37 in Leave, plus 4 foundational rules shared by both — every one traced to a specific, running code path.

One shared resolution engine

Employee > Shift > Designation > Grade > Department > Branch > Legal Entity > Company — the most specific configured rule always wins, for every policy type, in both modules.

One seam between modules

Leave writes exactly one kind of record — an approved leave day — into Attendance's own mirror table, at final approval, in the same database transaction.

One rule outranks everything

Once a pay period is locked, no punch, regularization, overtime approval, roster change, or leave sync can touch a date inside it — for any reason, by any role (Section 14).

How to Read This Document

This document is written to be read start to finish once, then used as a reference afterward.

For a client walkthrough or demo

  1. Walk through Sections 5–6 alongside a live demo — each step maps directly to something you can show on screen.
  2. Use Section 11 for the questions that come up most in practice: "what if someone is on leave and also worked overtime that day?"
  3. Keep Section 15 open as a lookup table for anything that comes up mid-conversation.

For configuring the system

  1. Use Sections 7–8 as the master list of every policy you can set, grouped by theme.
  2. Read Section 10 before assigning any policy at a scope narrower than Company — it explains exactly how the resolver picks a winner.
  3. Read Sections 13–14 before your first payroll cycle — they describe what happens to history when a policy changes or a period locks.

Every status tag in this document uses one of three plain labels: Active (this behaviour runs, exactly as described, today), By design (a deliberate choice, called out because it can otherwise surprise a new user), and Configurable, not yet wired (the setting exists and can be changed in the UI, but the calculation engine doesn't act on it yet — worth knowing before you rely on it).

04

System Overview

Both modules sit on the same foundation and meet at exactly one seam. Nothing below is aspirational — every box and arrow is a real, running code path, expanded in later sections.

FoundationAn 8-level scope walk (Employee > Shift > Designation > Grade > Department > Branch > Legal Entity > Company) resolves which policy version applies to a given employee, on a given date, for a given policy type. Both Attendance and Leave call the identical engine (Section 10).
Leave moduleOwns leave types, policies, the balance ledger, applications, and multi-level approval. On final approval, it writes one record — an approved day, half-day, or cancellation — into Attendance's own mirror table, inside the same database transaction as the approval itself.
Attendance moduleOwns the calendar. Combines punches, shift rules, holidays, weekly-offs, roster overrides and the leave day it received from Leave into one daily status, then separately detects overtime from the same punches.
OvertimeAttendance detects, classifies, caps and routes overtime for approval — entitlement only. No monetary amount is calculated in either module (Section 12).
Approval & lockRegularizations, overtime and leave each carry their own approval chain. Once a pay period is finalized and locked, it is read-only to every one of these paths — the single rule that outranks everything else in the system (Section 14).
PayrollA separate, future module. Attendance and Leave both stop at paid-day fractions, loss-of-pay days, and overtime entitlement minutes — converting those into money is that module's job, described in a companion document.
05

How Attendance Calculates a Day

Transcribed directly from the calculation engine's actual evaluation order (attendance/calculation.py::evaluate_day). Every step below runs, in this exact order, for every employee, every day.

We follow one employee, Ananya Rao, through a working example across all eight steps. Where a step depends on policy numbers your company configures (grace minutes, late bands, hour bands), we've used illustrative figures — labelled as such — to show how the mechanism works. The one number below that is an actual system default, not an illustration, is called out explicitly.

Step A‑01

Is the employee even in scope today?

What the system checks
Whether the date falls inside the employee's Employment.joining_date–last_working_day window. This reads the Employment record only — it deliberately does not look at whether the employee is on probation or confirmed.
If not in scope
Nothing below runs at all. If the employee is NOT_JOINED or EXITED for the date, there is no attendance day to calculate.
Example
Ananya joined on 2026-03-01 and has no exit date. For today's date, 2026-09-07, she is ACTIVE — the day proceeds to Step A-02.
Step A‑02

Is every setting needed to evaluate the day actually in place?

What the system checks
That a work calendar resolves for the employee's branch/company, and that every policy type (attendance, overtime, regularization) resolves via the 8-level scope walk. If either is missing, the day is marked CONFIG_ERROR and stops there.
Why it works this way
The system never silently assumes "Working" or "Present" when it doesn't actually know — a deliberate, fail-closed design used consistently throughout both modules.
If configuration is missing
The day is left in CONFIG_ERROR, visible to HR as a data-quality problem to resolve — assign a calendar or a policy at some scope that resolves for this employee.
Example
Ananya's branch has a holiday calendar assigned, and an Attendance Policy resolves for her at the Department scope. Configuration is complete — the day proceeds.
Step A‑03

Has HR specifically scheduled this person differently today?

What the system checks
Whether a published roster row exists for this employee and date marking it as a week-off override. If so, the day is NOT_EXPECTED — and this wins even if the calendar says it's an ordinary working day.
Overrides
The calendar's default working-day classification (Step A-05) — a schedule fact HR entered by name beats the calendar's general pattern.
Can be overridden further?
Only by a full-day approved leave later in the sequence (Step A-07).
Example
No roster override exists for Ananya on 2026-09-07 — the day proceeds under the calendar's default classification.
Step A‑04

Does a shift resolve for this employee today?

What the system checks
Whether a ShiftVersion resolves from the employee's base assignment (or a roster override, for the day's actual worked schedule). If none resolves, the missing-shift policy decides between ABSENT and INCOMPLETE.
Example
Ananya is assigned a Fixed shift, 09:30–18:30. The shift resolves — the day proceeds to the calendar check.
Step A‑05

What does the calendar say — holiday, weekly off, or working day?

What the system checks
The date against the assigned holiday calendar and weekly-off pattern. A calendar exception (shutdown, special-working, substitute or cancelled holiday) is checked first and can override the base pattern.
When a holiday lands on a weekly off
The day stays classified as weekly-off, with a holiday_on_weekly_off flag raised for visibility. The holiday does not promote or substitute the day — there is no automatic day-in-lieu. This is a deliberate design choice, not an oversight.
What can override this classification
A shift explicitly flagged to operate_on_holiday/operate_on_weekly_off, or a published roster override, both win over the calendar's default.
Example
2026-09-07 is an ordinary working Monday on Ananya's calendar — no holiday, no weekly-off. The day proceeds to punch evaluation.
Step A‑06

What do the punches say?

What the system checks
Raw punches are first paired (in/out), duplicate punches within a short window are collapsed, then worked minutes are run through the company's configured hour bands (Present / Half-Day / Absent by total minutes worked), late-arrival bands and early-departure bands (minutes late/early beyond a grace period), and, for flexible shifts, a core-hours breach check. None of these mutually suppress each other — any one can push the day to a worse status.
Configurable
Grace-in/out minutes (system default: 0 minutes unless your company sets otherwise), late/early bands, hour bands, duplicate-punch collapse window (default 60 seconds), core-hours breach handling.
If a punch is missing
Governed by missing_punch_behaviour — the system default is INCOMPLETE, deliberately not ABSENT. A zero-punch day never silently becomes Absent unless HR explicitly configures that harsher handling.
Result
A baseline attendance_status, worked minutes, late minutes, early-departure minutes, and any break/core-hours flags.
Example configuration (illustrative, not a system default)
Suppose Ananya's shift starts 09:30 with a 10-minute grace-in, and her policy's late bands are: 0–15 min late after grace → flagged only; 16–60 min → Half-Day; >60 min → Absent. Ananya punches in at 09:47 — 7 minutes late after the 10-minute grace window. That falls in the 0–15 band: she is flagged late, but her baseline status from the hour band (she worked a full day) stays Present.
Step A‑07

Is there approved leave for this day?

What the system checks
Whether an active Leave Day Projection exists for the employee and date — a record Attendance owns but Leave writes, the moment a leave application clears its final approval stage.
Overrides
Everything above it — including the calendar's NOT_EXPECTED classification from Step A-05 and the roster override from Step A-03. Under the default policy setting (KEEP_LEAVE), leave wins outright regardless of what punches say; late/early minutes are zeroed. Under the alternate PRESENT_CANCEL_LEAVE_HINT setting, the punch-derived status from Step A-06 is kept instead, and a flag is raised for Leave to see — but the leave application itself is never auto-cancelled by Attendance.
What it does not touch
The underlying calendar_day_type field is left exactly as Step A-05 set it. Overtime minutes detected in Step A-06 are not cleared — a day can carry both a Leave status and a nonzero overtime record (Section 11).
Example
No leave is approved for Ananya on 2026-09-07, so this step makes no change — her day stays Present, with a late flag, from Step A-06.
Step A‑08

Is there an approved on-duty record?

What the system checks
An approved ON_DUTY regularization (e.g. a client visit or official travel day) for the date.
Overrides
The punch-derived status from Step A-06, but loses to leave if both an approved leave and an approved on-duty record somehow cover the same date.
Example
No on-duty record exists — no change. Ananya's final calculated status for the day is Present, flagged late, worked minutes and any detected overtime as computed in Step A-06.

Regularization and Overtime are not steps in this chain. A regularization is never a "correction" applied on top of the sequence above — an approved regularization adds new, immutable punch records and the entire sequence A-01–A-08 re-runs against the new punch set. Overtime is a separate, parallel calculation that runs independently and is never itself overridden by any of the eight steps above (Section 12).

One rule sits outside this entire chain and outranks it: once the pay period covering this date is locked, none of Steps A-01–A-08 can execute at all for that date — not punches, not roster, not regularization, not leave-projection sync (Section 14).

06

How Leave Processes an Application

Transcribed directly from LeaveApplicationService.submit() and _approve_internal() — the actual code order. We follow a second employee, Rahul Mehta, applying for three days of Sick Leave, through submission and approval.

Step L‑01

Which policy applies, and does the calendar resolve?

What happens
The same 8-level scope walk used by Attendance resolves the LeavePolicyVersion for this employee, this leave type, this date. The work calendar is resolved for every date in the requested range.
If information is missing
Fails closed — submission is rejected outright if either the policy or the calendar doesn't resolve. No guessing.
Example
Rahul's Sick Leave policy resolves at the Department scope. The calendar resolves for his branch. Submission proceeds.
Step L‑02

Is the submitter allowed to submit this, for this person?

What happens
Checks whether the actual submitter (the employee themselves, their manager, or HR) is permitted to submit on this employee's behalf, per the resolved policy's on-behalf flags.
Example
Rahul is submitting for himself — allow_employee_request is enabled. Proceeds.
Step L‑03

Is the employee on probation, and does this leave type allow that?

What happens
Checks Employee.status directly — if PROBATION and the resolved policy's allowed_during_probation is false, the application is blocked outright, regardless of who submitted it (self, manager-on-behalf, or HR-on-behalf).
Cannot be bypassed by
An on-behalf submission — the check applies before that distinction matters. The only unblock path is confirming the employee to ACTIVE.
Worth knowing
Leave reads Employee.status for this check; Attendance never does — the two modules use independent signals for "is this employee active."
Example
Rahul was confirmed to ACTIVE six months ago. Proceeds.
Step L‑04

Is the request itself well-formed?

What happens
In order: half-day validity (only permitted for a single-date application), reason/attachment required if the policy demands it, the backdating window (max_backdated_days, unlimited if unset), and the monthly request cap (excluding cancelled applications).
Worth knowing
The backdating window only limits how far back a request can be submitted — it does not check whether the attendance period for that date is already locked. That's only checked later, at final approval (Step L-09).
Example
Rahul applies for three consecutive future dates, attaches no document (Sick Leave under 3 days doesn't require one under his company's policy), and this is his first request this month. All checks pass.
Step L‑05

Which requested dates actually consume balance?

What happens
Each date in the range is classified: WORKING/SPECIAL_WORKING days consume leave; WEEKLY_OFF/HOLIDAY/SHUTDOWN days do not — unless the sandwich rule applies (a non-consuming day bracketed on both sides by consuming leave days within the same application is pulled back into consumption).
Configurable
Sandwich rule (sandwich_rule_enabled, default true). The look-back only ever considers dates within this one application — it never looks at a separately-submitted adjacent application.
If nothing consumes
Fails closed — a request whose entire range resolves to zero consuming days is rejected as an invalid date range.
Example
Rahul's three requested days are all ordinary working days — no holiday or weekly-off inside the range, so the sandwich rule is irrelevant here. All 3 days consume balance.
Step L‑06

Does this clash with another leave type already booked?

What happens
Checked only against an explicit LeaveCombinationRule row with allowed=False between this leave type and another type the employee already has active on an overlapping date. Absence of a rule means the combination is allowed. This only ever governs different leave types — two overlapping applications of the same type are not blocked by any rule today (Section 9).
Example
Rahul has no other active leave application on these dates. Proceeds.
Step L‑07

Is there enough balance?

What happens
Balance is not a stored number — it's the live sum of every entry in the employee's append-only leave ledger. If reserve_balance_on_submission is enabled, other pending (SUBMITTED) applications of the same type are netted first.
Can be overridden?
No. The balance check is a hard block, unconditionally, for every submitter type — negative balance is never allowed.
Example
Rahul's Sick Leave ledger shows a running balance of 8.0 days. Requesting 3.0 leaves 5.0 remaining — sufficient. Proceeds to approval-chain resolution.
Step L‑08

Who approves, and in what order?

What happens
The configured chain (AUTO / MANAGER / MANAGER_THEN_HR / MANAGER_THEN_HOD_THEN_HR / HR) resolves real identities as of the submission date. Any stage whose identity can't be resolved (no manager on file, employee is their own department head) is silently dropped; if every stage is unresolved, the chain falls back to a single HR stage.
Always-on rule
Self-approval is blocked unconditionally at every stage — no policy setting can re-enable it.
Example
Rahul's policy is MANAGER_THEN_HR. His manager is resolved — the application enters SUBMITTED, routed to his manager first.
Step L‑09

Approval: staged vs. final

What happens
A non-final stage approval only advances approval_stage — no ledger or attendance effect yet. Only the final stage's approval triggers a ledger debit (unless the leave type doesn't require balance) and syncs the approved day(s) into Attendance's Leave Day Projection, inside the same database transaction as the approval write.
What can block final approval
If the attendance period covering any of the dates is already closed, the sync is refused and the entire approval transaction rolls back — the application stays SUBMITTED, never partially approved (Section 14).
Example
Rahul's manager approves — stage advances to HR, no ledger effect yet. HR then approves: this is the final stage. The attendance period for these dates is still open, so the ledger debits 3.0 days and the projection syncs into Attendance in one atomic step. Rahul's balance is now 5.0; Attendance will show those three dates as LEAVE the next time it calculates them.

Rejection is terminal at every stage. If HR rejects after the manager already approved, the rejection stands — there is no "send it back" step, and no ledger or attendance effect occurs.

Cancellation is the only reversal. There is no separate "withdraw" or "amend" action. The original submitter (only) can cancel a SUBMITTED application with no side effect, or an APPROVED one, which reverses the ledger debit and cancels the Attendance projection — but only while the attendance period is still open. If it's closed, cancellation is refused the same way approval is (Section 14).

Precedence: What Wins When Two Rules Apply

Every meaningful place two rules could both apply to the same day or the same request, and which one currently wins.

Attendance — policy vs. policy

SituationCurrent behaviour
A public holiday falls on a weekly-off dayWeekly Off classification stands, flagged; the holiday doesn't promote or substitute the day.
A shift is explicitly flagged to operate on a holiday/weekly-offThe shift's "operates" flag wins over the calendar's default.
Calendar says Weekly Off, but a published roster override says WorkingRoster wins — a named schedule fact beats the calendar's general pattern.
Roster moves someone to a different shift for the day, and that shift has its own resolved policyRoster wins for the actual worked schedule. Policy resolution (grace minutes, bands, etc.) still uses the employee's base shift assignment for that day, not the roster shift.
An employee-, branch-, or shift-scoped policy vs. a broader-scope policyThe more specific scope always wins (8-level walk).
An approved regularization vs. the original punchesBoth persist — regularization adds new punch records, originals are never edited or deleted.
Approved leave and detected overtime, same dayBoth are simply recorded as true, independently — leave does not clear detected OT minutes (Section 11).

The Scope Hierarchy — How "Which Policy Applies" Is Decided

There is exactly one scope-resolution hierarchy, reused by every policy in both modules, and it runs from most specific to least specific — not top-down by organisational seniority. There is no separate "Employment Category" scope tier; Grade and Designation are the closest equivalents.

The one hierarchy — most specific wins

  1. Employee-specific assignment — a policy set for this one person by name
  2. Shift — the policy tied to the employee's assigned shift
  3. Designation
  4. Grade
  5. Department
  6. Branch
  7. Legal Entity
  8. Company (the fallback, least specific) every policy type resolves through this exact order

Sitting outside this ladder entirely, in ascending order of authority:

1. An approved transaction (a regularization or OT approval, a leave approval) is not a competing policy assignment — it's a one-time action that executes strictly within whatever the resolved policy already allows. It cannot approve an OT amount above the configured cap, or a regularization type outside the policy's allowed list.

2. HR manual actions (approving, rejecting, publishing a new policy version, reopening a period) are the same kind of transaction-level action as above — not a distinct "override" layer with its own precedence. The one place HR has unilateral, unscoped authority is publishing a more specific policy assignment, which is just the ladder above working as designed.

3. A locked, historical record beats every single thing above it, including HR manual actions. This is the one rule with no exception anywhere in either module (Section 14).

Where Attendance and Leave Meet

What happens when Attendance and Leave both have an opinion about the same day. Every scenario below is backed by a passing test or an explicit, cited code path.

Leave

Decides whether someone is approved to be away, for how much of the day, and whether it's paid.

→writes once, at final approval, into one shared table — inside the same transaction

Attendance

Owns the calendar. Combines that decision with punches, holidays and shift rules into the day's final record.

Leave never writes an Attendance table directly, and Attendance never writes a Leave table directly — the entire connection is this one seam, called the Leave Day Projection. If Attendance refuses the write (most commonly: the period is locked), the whole Leave-side transaction rolls back and the application is left exactly as it was before — never partially approved.

Further interactions worth knowing

Comp-off: Attendance emits, Leave decides

Attendance records approved OT convertible to time-off as a read-only fact (minutes + expiry date). Leave's own sweep converts it to balance using its configured day-length — and skips entirely, rather than guessing, if that isn't configured. A clean one-directional handoff.

Re-syncing the same application is idempotent

If Leave re-syncs the same application ID (e.g. after a correction), the existing projection row updates in place — no duplicates, and flipping paid/unpaid correctly flips the paid/LOP split.

Overlapping projections are rejected

If active leave fractions for one employee-date would sum above 1.00, the sync is refused outright — fail-closed, not a silent overwrite.

Cancelling reverts to the punch-derived truth

Cancel a projection on a day with real punches, and the status dynamically reverts to whatever the punches alone produce — the overlay is recomputed live, never cached.

PROBATION means different things to each module

Attendance derives "is this person active" purely from Employment dates and never reads confirmation status; Leave reads confirmation status directly for probation checks. No conflict has been observed between the two.

Backdated leave can be submitted for an already-locked date

Nothing checks the attendance lock at submission — only at final approval. An application can sit Submitted before failing at approval time.

Not an interaction: Leave never overwrites the underlying calendar day-type (holiday/weekly-off/working) — only the displayed attendance status and paid/LOP fractions change.

Overtime: From Detection to Approval

Architectural boundary: Attendance determines and records overtime entitlement — duration, classification, and approval. Payroll is responsible for the final monetary overtime amount, using the employee's actual wage and statutory/company payroll rules. Attendance does not, and by design should not, calculate money. See the companion document on the Attendance→Payroll fact contract for full detail on that boundary.

Eligibility

Governed by an eligibility mode: Never and Shift-flagged are both fully wired. Always and Grade-based currently both mean "always eligible" — a real grade check isn't wired in yet, so if grade-based OT eligibility matters to you, flag it before relying on that setting.

Calculation

Worked minutes beyond the shift's scheduled end are the starting point. A configurable minimum-minutes threshold discards trivial excess; the remainder is rounded per policy, then clamped against daily, weekly and monthly caps. The weekly/monthly caps look at sibling records for the period, so recomputing one day's OT after the fact can shift slightly if neighbouring days have also changed — an expected characteristic of period-based capping, not a defect.

Classification

Each OT record is classified by day-class — Normal, Holiday, Weekly-Off, or Night — which determines which informational rate row applies for later payroll reference. Holiday and Weekly-Off classification are driven directly by the calendar. Night classification currently comes only from the employee's shift being flagged as crossing midnight, not from the actual punch clock-time — overtime worked late at night on an ordinary day-shift is not classified as Night today.

Approval

Follows the same manager/HR approval pattern as regularization, with self-approval blocked unconditionally at every stage. OT is detected and recorded after the fact; a "requires pre-approval" setting exists but doesn't change this yet.

Caps & overrides

Daily/weekly/monthly caps clamp the detected amount before it ever reaches an approver — there is no per-request waiver path around a configured cap. A terminal OT record (approved, auto-approved, or rejected) is never revised by a later recompute, even if the OT policy changes afterward.

Comp-off — the boundary with Leave

When OT is approved as "time off instead of pay," Attendance emits the converted minutes and an expiry date as a read-only fact. Leave's own comp-off sweep converts that into leave balance days, using its own configured day-length — consistent with the fail-closed principle throughout this system, it skips the conversion entirely rather than guessing if that day-length isn't configured.

This document deliberately does not describe a universal statutory multiplier (such as "2×") — the informational rate rows in the system are configurable per company and are not tied to any specific jurisdiction's statutory requirement.

Locking a Pay Period

The record lifecycle is simpler than a typical Draft→Submitted→Approved→Processed→Finalized→Locked→Reopened→Adjusted chain. What exists is:

OPENAn attendance period's default state. Punches, regularizations, roster changes and leave-projection syncs all execute freely, subject only to their own individual policies.
VALIDATED / VALIDATION_FAILEDFinalize first runs a validation pass against six typed blocking conditions (e.g. unresolved INCOMPLETE days under a policy that requires them resolved). Any single blocker fails the whole attempt, atomically — nothing is left half-finalized.
CLOSEDThe period is locked. This is the one rule that outranks everything else in the entire system. No punch, no regularization (submission or approval), no OT approval, no roster change, and no leave approval or cancellation can touch a date inside a closed period — not for any reason, not for any role.
REOPENEDA deliberately separate, higher-permission action from closing, requiring a reason. Reopening only removes the lock — it does not restore anything or recompute anything automatically. Days become mutable again, but any day HR doesn't manually recompute keeps whatever numbers it had at the moment it was locked, even if a policy has since changed underneath it.

Can a locked day be changed?

Not without first reopening the whole period. There is no single-record "fix just this one day" mechanism — the unit of unlock is the entire period.

Who can lock / unlock?

Two distinct, separately-grantable permissions. Locking requires passing validation; unlocking requires a stated reason. The separation is deliberate — reopening is treated as the riskier action.

Is there an audit trail?

Yes — every lock-lifecycle transition (Opened, Validated, Validation Failed, Closed, Reopened) is logged, as is every regularization/OT/leave approval action and every calendar-exception or roster-override edit.

Does reopening trigger recompute?

No. A reopened period can carry stale numbers indefinitely if nobody recomputes the affected days manually — there is no system-enforced "reopen checklist" today.

Manual correction and regularization are the same feature. There is no distinct "manual correction" path separate from the regularization workflow — every correction to punches or status flows through regularization. An approved regularization doesn't edit anything; it adds new, immutable punch records and triggers a full recalculation of the day in the same transaction.
